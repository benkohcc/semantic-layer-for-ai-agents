Baseline Tennis Co. · a semantic layer, explained

Same AI. Same data. Different answers.

An AI agent with raw access to your data answers every question confidently, and some answers are wrong in ways nobody catches. A semantic layer is the written-down meaning that sits between the agent and the data, and it is the difference below.

The agent only ever touches the top of the cake. A question climbs down and back up the same stack: tools call engines, engines consult the written knowledge, the knowledge governs how the raw tiers are read. The dotted path skips the stack and lands 44 percent wrong, because everything that made the answer right lives in the glowing tier, and raw SQL never passes through it.

Right numbersExclusions live inside the metric. There is no path that skips them.
Current documentsA registry tracks what is in force. Stale policies cannot pose as current.
Honest refusalsDeclared gaps make “we do not have that” a guaranteed answer, not a hope.
Extend by writingNew metrics and capabilities are knowledge files, not engineering projects.

The build

The insight lives in files, and each one fires at a different moment

The same four insights from above, moved out of chat threads and memories into files a person can read and a machine can enforce. These are real excerpts from this build, each paired with the moment it fires and the error it prevents.

metrics/net_revenue.yaml 
formula: SUM(gross_amount - refund_amount)
filters:
  - status  = 'completed'
  - channel != 'wholesale'
# test rows are QA artifacts; wholesale is a
# separate business line. Together they inflate
# revenue by roughly a third.
seasonal: December runs below band by design
caveat:   net of refunds; finance reports gross
Used when computing & interpreting $615,188 → $425,966

The filters strip the rows that were never marketing revenue, every time, because there is no path around them. And the number arrives interpreted: within its band, with the December note and the gross-vs-net caveat attached.

catalog/document_registry.yaml 
refund-policy-2024:
  status: draft   # never adopted
  superseded_by: refund-policy-2026
  note: circulated in 2024 and abandoned.
        NOTHING IN THE DOCUMENT SAYS SO:
        a draft reads exactly like a policy.
Used when retrieving documents the 2024 draft → the policy in force

The abandoned draft scores high on similarity, because it reads like a confident policy. The registry outranks similarity, so the answer cites the 2026 policy and names the stale draft instead of quoting it.

playbooks/metric-decline-diagnosis.md 
Step 1: confirm the change is real
Step 2: check seasonality BEFORE looking
        for a cause
"Compare to the same month last year, not
 to the adjacent month. A December decline

Used when reasoning, step by step “revenue is crashing” → “December dips by design”

“Why did revenue drop?” follows a written procedure instead of improvisation, so a designed seasonal dip stops being diagnosed as a crisis every single year.

ontology/concepts.yaml

# the decline wins over any adjacent number

“What’s our NPS?” resolves to a declared gap before any search runs. The refusal is guaranteed by the file, and repeat rate is offered as adjacent evidence, never dressed up as satisfaction.

This is why extending the layer is writing, not engineering: a new metric is a new YAML file, a new question type is a new playbook. The engines that execute these files never change.

Two capabilities worth seeing

It knows which document is current

Documents do not declare their own status, so the layer keeps a registry that tracks it externally, the way a CMS or an approval workflow does. Three documents from the corpus; all three read as current policy.

In force

Refund Policy

“Customers may return equipment within 60 days of delivery for a full refund…”

Never adopted

Refund Policy

“Customers may return equipment within 30 days. A 10 percent restocking fee applies…”

Superseded

Stringing SLA

“Standard restring turnaround is five business days from drop-off…”

Nothing in any of these files says which one is in force. Reading harder will not tell you.

The harder case is the third card: both versions of that SLA were genuinely in force at different times. Neither is wrong. Only the effective date separates them, and only the registry knows it.

It says “I don’t know” when it should

The layer declares what the business does not have, so an honest refusal is guaranteed rather than hoped for.

“What’s our NPS?”

Declined, correctly

No satisfaction, sentiment, or NPS data is collected anywhere in this business. The nearest available signals are repeat purchase rate and refund rate, and neither measures sentiment: a customer can be unhappy and still repurchase.

The boundary is precise: “are we out of stock?” is answerable, “how long have we been out?” is not, because no movement history exists. Confident nonsense erodes trust faster than “I don’t know” ever does; reliable refusal is what makes the reliable answers usable.

Why it matters

The business case in four lines

Wrong answers are expensive and invisible

A 44 percent overstatement does not announce itself. Someone builds a forecast on it, and the failure surfaces a quarter later.

Definitions drift without one

Three teams asking “what is revenue” get three numbers. The layer makes the governed definition the only one an agent can reach.

Trust compounds, in both directions

A tool that occasionally invents an answer gets abandoned, because checking it costs more than doing the work yourself.

The knowledge already exists, in heads

Someone knows about the test rows. Someone knows December always dips. Written down, it survives them leaving. In code, it does not.

How it works

What happens to a question

1

What do you mean? · routing

“How much did we make” maps to the governed revenue metric. A question about something the business does not track maps to a declared gap, which produces a refusal with a reason.

2

How should this be done? · playbooks

A “why did this drop” question requires checking seasonality before hunting for a cause, or December gets diagnosed as a crisis every year. The layer supplies the procedure.

3

Compute it correctly · governed paths

The metrics engine applies the exclusions. The registry ranks the documents. Neither depends on the agent remembering anything.

4

What does it mean? · interpretation

The number travels with its band, its direction of goodness, its caveats, and its confidence.

4 · The interface

Knowledge outweighs engine

Written knowledge~3,800 lines
Engine code~2,900 lines

The split matters more than the ratio: not one line of the engine knows what revenue means. Adding a metric means writing a definition file; adding a question type means writing a playbook. Both were done during this build with no program change.

Several stores, one rule

Relational: exact and additive

“Revenue in July, by channel” is arithmetic over rows. But rows cannot tell you what a policy means.

Vector: meaning survives paraphrase

“Can customers send things back” will never keyword-match “Refund and Returns Policy.” Embeddings make the match survive the rewording.

Document graph: how things relate

“Which policies apply to racket discounting” lives in no single passage; the answer is in how documents relate. Customer chains (“referrals of referrals”) need no extra store: they run as recursive queries on the warehouse itself.

build_audience search_campaigns
select populations ↓ hand back a reference
get_metric · the metrics engine computes

“Graphs and audience builders select populations. Only the metrics engine computes.”

However a population was found, its number comes through the same governed definition. That is what keeps several stores from producing three versions of the truth.

The interface

One connection, nine tools

You never choose between them: you ask in plain language and the layer routes it. “Search the documents for our revenue” is a metric question wearing a disguise, and the layer knows it.

Ten is deliberately small: one governed way to get a number, one to reach a document. The two marked selects hand off, and the number always comes from get_metric: the one rule, made visible in the interface.

Boundaries

What it is not

×A copy of your data

It stores meaning and pointers, and reads your warehouse in place.

×Documentation

Documentation is for humans, optional, and goes stale quietly. This is machine-enforced and travels with every result.

×A replacement for analysts

It encodes what they already know, so that knowledge scales past their calendars and survives them leaving.

×One giant company-wide ontology

Those projects fail. This is a small shared spine with self-contained domain packages hanging off it.

The short version

Raw data plus a capable AI produces confident answers, some of which are wrong, and you cannot tell which from looking at them. The same AI plus a semantic layer produces answers that are correct, carry their own caveats, and refuse honestly when the data does not support the question.

The difference is not the model. It is whether the meaning was written down.
